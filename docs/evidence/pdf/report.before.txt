 High CPR (> 1.0) inside permanent shadow regions indicates the Coherent Backscatter
Opposition Effect (CBOE), characteristic of low-loss dielectric media such as water ice.

5. SCIENTIFIC LIMITATIONS & MISSION ASSUMPTIONS
1. 
Radar Signatures:
 Polarimetric anomalies (CPR > 1.0, DOP < 0.13) are consistent with potential subsurface ice, but do not constitute
certified ground truth confirmation without in-situ drilling or neutron spectrometry.
2. 
No ML model:
 the Random Forest ice-likelihood classifier was WITHDRAWN. It was fitted to uniformly random labels whose positive
class was defined as CPR 1.05-2.5  a range this amplitude-only product cannot reach  so every probability it produced was an
extrapolation from fabricated examples. No probability appears in this report.
3. 
Volumetric uncertainty:
 ice volume is a 3-tier range over assumed regolith stratigraphy (conservative 2 m / 0.05, expected 5 m / 0.15,
upper 10 m / 0.3). The area is measured; the depth and the pore fraction are assumptions and are stated with every figure.
4. 
Rover energy:
 a simplified engineering estimate for a 30 kg micro-rover, not a substitute for certified NASA/ISRO flight dynamic
models. The planner is a uniform-cost (Dijkstra) search; no optimality beyond that is claimed.
5. 
Reproducibility:
 every figure above is computed from the Chandrayaan-2 DFSAR product named on page 1, and re-running the
pipeline on that product reproduces them. Any quantity that could not be computed is printed as NO DATA rather than filled in.
